Read the figure legends and limits

Mask panels: orange means detected fire, pale means encoded background, and gray means deliberately hidden input. Error panels use their own hit/false-alarm/miss legend. Feature panels show raw stored values with independent 2nd–98th percentile color limits, not natural-color photos. Zoom changes display size only. No probability values are inferred from PNG colors; exact metric counts are unavailable in this partial export.

A zero can mean no detected fire, a preprocessing fill value, or a deliberately hidden observation. Its interpretation depends on provenance.

Try it yourself

A cloud-obscured input is filled with zero. Does that establish no fire?

Reason through the question and every option

How to reason: Ask why this cell contains zero. It may be a measured or encoded background value, or it may be a placeholder inserted after hiding an observation. Those histories carry different information even when the stored numbers match.

  • Yes — not the answer. This mistakes an encoding choice for a physical observation. Replacing an unknown value with zero tells us nothing about whether fire was present under the hidden input.
  • No; absence of observation is not evidence of absence — correct. A missing observation leaves the underlying state unknown. Use the declared corruption rule and any available validity information to distinguish missing input from observed background.

Take away: Keep the input mask and target separate. Hiding an input is not permission to relabel its target as no fire; observed background itself still means no detection in its label window.

Our current experiments impose controlled missingness on prepared inputs. They do not fully reconstruct natural cloud, smoke, delay and quality mechanisms.

Done when: you can explain why the model and evaluator need a clear missingness contract.

Try it yourself

Can official Fold 2 AP serve as the project’s frozen-foundation score?

Reason through the question and every option

How to reason: An improvement claim needs a comparator evaluated under the same contract. Here official Fold 2 and project B3 differ in training years, preprocessing, initialization and training setup. Their score difference combines these changes.

  • Yes, both use Res18-U-Net — not the answer. The architecture specifies only part of the experiment. Two Res18-U-Net models can see different data, start from different weights and be scored on different populations; their raw score difference cannot isolate continuation.
  • No, data, initialization and protocol differ — correct. Use the identified project B3 as row A and evaluate it on the same samples, masks and metric procedure as row B. This gives the continuation a relevant starting-point comparison.

Take away: Retain the official result as reproduction evidence. Do not copy it into the project’s row A merely because the model names match.

Done when: you can list at least two reasons the scores cannot be substituted.

Step 4 · Prepare the foundation

A named checkpoint is not enough.

Your task: Identify the assets needed for the separate project protocol.

The T1 missingness-trained foundation (B3) is the shared starting checkpoint for our continuation exercise. Its T5 counterpart is B5; T5 also changes architecture and features.

  1. Prepare the project environment and corrected data.
  2. Identify training-only normalization statistics.
  3. Use an instructor-provided foundation with checksum/provenance, or follow the foundation training and export guide. For the T1 exercise, only B3 is needed.
  4. Record the actual starting weights; do not substitute an ERM continuation checkpoint.

B3 is selected by its foundation-training validation rule. The later ERM/cosine continuations use their final-step rule. Foundation selection and continuation selection are different stages.

Done when: you can locate and verify data, statistics and the exact foundation checkpoint.
