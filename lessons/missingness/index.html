Step 1 · Distinguish

A zero is not an explanation.

A zero can mean no detected fire, a preprocessing fill value, or a deliberately hidden observation. Its interpretation depends on provenance.

Try it yourself

A cloud-obscured input is filled with zero. Does that establish no fire?

Check your reasoning

A fill value does not establish the underlying fire state. Keep missingness semantics separate from the physical interpretation of a zero.

Our current experiments impose controlled missingness on prepared inputs. They do not fully reconstruct natural cloud, smoke, delay and quality mechanisms.

Done when: you can explain why the model and evaluator need a clear missingness contract.

Step 4 · Prepare the foundation

A named checkpoint is not enough.

The T1 missingness-trained foundation (B3) is the shared starting checkpoint for our continuation exercise. Its T5 counterpart is B5; T5 also changes architecture and features.

  1. Prepare the project environment and corrected data.
  2. Identify training-only normalization statistics.
  3. Use an instructor-provided foundation with checksum/provenance, or follow the foundation training and export guide. For the T1 exercise, only B3 is needed.
  4. Record the actual starting weights; do not substitute an ERM continuation checkpoint.

B3 is selected by its foundation-training validation rule. The later ERM/cosine continuations use their final-step rule. Foundation selection and continuation selection are different stages.

Done when: you can locate and verify data, statistics and the exact foundation checkpoint.
